# Daybrief privacy

Daybrief works on your phone. It has no account, no server, no ads and no analytics.

## What it reads, and why

Only for the parts you switch on:

| Part | Reads | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Messages | Your notifications | To turn them into questions and reminders in your brief |
| Phone calendar | Your calendar | To show your events next to your tasks, and (if you choose) add reminders to it |
| People | Contacts and call history | To show when you last talked to someone |
| Health | Sleep, steps, heart rate and workouts from Health Connect | To show them in the app and widgets |
| Saved | Your screenshots (to notice a new one) | To offer to save it |
| Edge lighting | Notifications, and which app is on screen | To light the edge and stay quiet while you use certain apps |

## Where it goes

All of it stays in Daybrief's private storage on your phone. Nothing is sent to the developer or anyone else.

The app only connects to the internet to:
- show previews of links you save: it asks that website (or TikTok, YouTube and Instagram's preview service for their links) for the page title and picture. If that fails, it opens the page invisibly in a web view to read them, and the page may load its own content like any website,
- check this GitHub page for a new version (automatically, at most every 12 hours, when you open the app),
- and save a backup where you choose (the backup is encrypted with your password).

## Removing your data

Uninstalling Daybrief deletes everything it stored. Turning a part off in Settings → Features stops it reading anything new.
